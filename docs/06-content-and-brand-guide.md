## Voice and tone

1. **The people speak about you.** All world text is in their voice. Menus stay neutral and plain.
2. **The voice shifts each Dispensation.** Dawn terse and elemental; the Law formal; the Fulness modern and skeptical.
3. **Dry wit lives in card flavor text.** One short line per card.
4. **Suffering is shown, not told.** Ink spreads, light dims, music falters.
5. **What they call you depends on their religion.** The game never genders you.
6. **Failure is haunting.** Apostasy and Collapse end on the people's last words.
7. **The game does not preach.** The player's choices decide what is right.
8. **Never mention faith outside the fiction.**

## How each Dispensation worships (added 2026-11-22, in review)

**We do not set the names. The people do.** In every Dispensation the people's model chooses what to call the player and how to speak to them, and it can differ from run to run. What the game supplies is a clue card per option: the kind of god or gods these people would believe in and the manner of their worship. The Voice (LLM or template fallback) and the Spark model both receive the clue card. The names in the last column are only examples of what the people might arrive at, kept for testing and for the template fallback's word lists. They are invented, and none is a real name of God or a line of real scripture.

Rules:

- The clue card never contains a name. It describes the kind of worship: how many gods, how near or far, what they are thought to care about, how they are approached.
- The chosen name shows up in prayer bubbles, in the Testament the people write, in witness bios and in Trial text. Menus and tutorials just say "you".
- The name can change inside a Dispensation when the people change. A wrong or splintered name is a signal to the player about the Truth dial, not an error.
- Closeness follows the story: plain and near at the start, formal, crowded or lost in the middle, plain and near again at the end. The clues lean that way; the people may still surprise us.
- The template fallback picks from a small list of name parts per option, with the seeded RNG, so it also varies by run.

## Cards and suits

- Play exactly like Balatro. Ranks 2–10 are plain numbered cards; 1, 11, 12, 13 are special.
- Suits keep their names; symbols are hand gestures: Command = raised open palm, Punish = closed fist, Inspire = upturned offering palm, Reveal = hand drawing back a curtain.
- Proposed rank names: 1 **Origin** (set in deep black), 11 **Herald**, 12 **Elder**, 13 **Sovereign**. Face figures are ungendered silhouettes.
- Cards show exact numbers (Witness value and signed dial effects). Flavor text is one short line. Card backs change each Dispensation.
- Enhancement tiers: gold embroidery edge → etched plate → pure white porcelain.
- Godhood Powers: tall portrait cards. Keys: seals or sigils. Tablets: stained glass panes.
- Big combos: full Balatro-style spectacle with screen shake.

## Art direction, round 3 (2026-11-20)

Bemorte's direction after the first UI round. These override anything above that conflicts.

- **Cohesion over numerical clarity.** Prefer a shape, a sweep or a material to a number.
- **Cards carry rank and suit only.** Witness values and dial effects live in press-and-hold tooltips, as in Balatro. Godhood Power faces carry only a seal and a name.
- **Give things room.** Smaller type, more space, fewer things per screen.
- **Logo:** clearly a planet seen from above, in an iOS-style layered icon. Lit in gold from the upper right, city lights on the night side. This replaces the abstract Veil mark.
- **Type:** should evoke temple inscriptions and the labels of distilled products. Cinzel (engraved capitals) for names and ranks, Cormorant Garamond for text and numbers, Jost for tiny tracked labels.
- **Materials must look real:** burnished gold with a soft sheen and fine grain (never streaky leaf), gold embroidery, etched brass plate, embossed porcelain, obsidian, leaded glass lit from behind.
- **Stained glass has a voice:** each suit has its own glass pattern (Command columns, Punish chevrons, Inspire a diamond lattice with lights coming on, Reveal sunburst), and the limb of the world runs along the foot of every card.
- **Emblems** are solid gold shapes with cut-out detail, built on the lancet arch. Nothing simple or childish.
- **Dials** read as seals with a sweeping ring, not numbers.
- **Suit emblems are whole hands** (round 4): Command a raised open palm, Punish a closed fist, Inspire a hand pointing up at a spark (the moment of aha, replacing the offering palm), Reveal a hand gripping a curtain drawn aside with a star behind it. The hand is always the largest thing in the emblem.
- **Inspire versus Reveal:** Inspire is inspiration, discovery, the moment of aha. Reveal is being shown and awed.
- **Court cards** (Herald, Elder, Sovereign) are hooded figures in black lacquer and gold line with an empty hood: a horn, a lamp on a staff, a crown and rays.

### Round 5 (2026-11-21): sculpted objects

- Nothing is a flat sign. Hands, court figures, seals, dial emblems and Watch buildings are modelled as solid objects lit from the upper left, with rounded relief, a caught highlight and a soft cast shadow. Flat fills are kept for text and for emblems under 16 px.
- The four suit hands follow reference hand drawings (Font Awesome Free solid hands, CC BY 4.0; credit in the app's acknowledgements) and are recast as sculpted gold: open palm, raised fist, one finger raised to a spark, a fist drawing a curtain aside.
- Herald, Elder and Sovereign are hooded statues in deep indigo lacquer with cast gold, on a gold plinth, built from separate lit forms. The hood stays empty.
- Watch blocks keep their three hues but gain soft face shading, a lit top edge and cast shadows. Buildings get recessed doors and windows, turned domes and silos.

### Item review (2026-11-20 evening): Logo and Materials

- Logo: the lit rim and flare on the planet are porcelain white, never gold.
- Gold is shown as embroidery and finery (gold thread couched on night silk, seed pearls), not as crinkled leaf.
- Enhancement tier two is etched plates: thin engraved brass, hand engraved with invented characters, no rings. Not modern brushed steel.
- The mark on materials is the seal: two squares laid across each other as an eight-point star, with a circle at the heart.
- Creeping ink soaks in and feathers like ink drunk by a page. Soft glow is clean, quiet light from above.
- These still need to be carried through cards, buttons and the shop in the whole-system pass.
- Card review: Reveal's emblem is only a hand: the open hand reaching down from above with a spark of light beneath it (no curtain, no props; a palm-up hand was tried and was unreadable). Herald, Elder and Sovereign keep the hooded-figure concept (teardrop hood with an empty face, short cape with gold piping and a star clasp, long pleated cloak, round plinth; horn, lamp-staff, crown with ring of light) and are built as real 3D models rendered with studio light, in carved walnut, white marble and cast gold. Flat or drawn figures are out. The bottom-right rank tab is upright. A selected card takes a tight neon edge, not a wide glow.
- Suit hands: the four hands in the card medallions are sculpted gold reliefs (the earlier cast look), not 3D renders and not flat icons. Both a full 3D cast and a flat traced icon were tried and reverted.
- Court figures: Herald, Elder and Sovereign are flat vector icons traced from their 3D models in three flat tones, no gradients or gloss. The 3D models remain the master for proportion.
- **Twelve court figures (Nov 22 2026).** The rank still sets the material (Herald walnut, Elder white marble, Sovereign gold) and the suit now sets the leader, so the twelve court cards show twelve kinds of religious leader. Heralds: The Caller (shawl, ram's horn; Command), The Herald (hood, long horn; Punish), The Novice (shaved head, sash, beads, bell; Inspire), The Shell Caller (leaf crown, garland, sea shell; Reveal). Elders: The Scholar (turban, beard, open book; Command), The Sage (topknot, thin beard, lantern staff; Punish), The Elder (hood, lamp staff; Inspire), The Veiled (long veil, lit lamp; Reveal). Sovereigns: The Anointed (domed cap, plate of twelve stones, hands lifted; Command), The Shepherd (domed crown, long beard, stole, crook; Punish), The Sun Keeper (feather fan, collar, sun disc; Inspire), The Sovereign (crown, ring of light; Reveal). These supersede the all-hooded rule above: every figure stays faceless (an empty hood or a smooth bare head), and none carries a real emblem of any faith or anything cross-like. The look names are working labels; rank names are unchanged.
- Dials review: no dial emblem may read as a cross. Redemption is a fish (the lit doorway read as a cross). Agency is the forked path turned on its side so it reads as a timeline that branches left to right. Collapse is a skull, for death. Watch currencies rank like medals and always appear in this order: Devotion gold, Insight silver, Plenty bronze, Harmony green.
- Buttons review: gold buttons are even gold cloth (one smooth sheen, fine regular weave, no streaks) with porcelain rims and inner lines instead of gold ones. Their lettering is heavy, outlined in porcelain, and glows. Lacquer (black) buttons keep their look with bolder lettering.
- Shop review: prices are set in plain bold sans numerals in pale gold, never in the serif or display face; decorative lettering made prices hard to read. Leave clear space between the star rule and item names.
- Seals review: the three-of-a-kind hand and its Key are named Presidency, never Trinity (not a term this cosmology uses). No emblem may be a single tall rounded shaft: towers, pillars and spires are drawn broad, pointed and grouped. Devotion and Temple Builder are a broad house with three pointed spires, Pillar of Fire is a wide flame on a ground line, Many Mansions is three gabled houses.
