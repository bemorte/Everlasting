# 02 — Cosmology and story

> Status: Draft v0.1 · Source: answers 2a–2c

## Tone (2a: C)

Everlasting is **aligned with Latter-day Saint cosmology but never announces it.** A player from the faith should recognize the shape of the plan, the dispensations, the veil and the degrees of glory and feel it was handled with care. A player from outside should simply experience a thoughtful, philosophical god game about agency, opposition and redemption.

Writing rules:
1. **Use the idea, not the term.** "The Council Before" instead of the premortal council; "the Light" instead of the Light of Christ; "Keys" without "priesthood".
2. **No real scripture quotes, no real prophets' names, no church names or logos.** Prophets are procedurally named people from your world.
3. **Doctrine shapes the rules, not the dialogue.** The most faithful elements live in the mechanics (agency costs, the veil, ordinances for the dead) rather than in text.
4. **Respect, never parody.** The Shadow is an optional mechanic, not a mockery. Religions your people invent can be corrupt, but true principles are never played for laughs.
5. **Welcoming to all.** Players with other beliefs, or none, should find the game's questions open rather than preachy.

The left column below is **internal reference only** (for the team and for Claude). It never appears in the shipped game.

## The Eight Dispensations

Each is a distinct visual style (doc 03) and a different set of world conditions. Names are in-game names; the internal reference sits in parentheses for the team.

| # | Dispensation | (Internal ref) | World state | Signature Trial (boss Era) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dawn** | Adam | One small family-clan, a garden edge, stone tools | *The First Rivalry*: a feud between brothers splits the clan |
| 2 | **The Walking City** | Enoch | Villages, first cities, early writing | *The Lifted City*: one settlement nears One Heart; the rest grow resentful |
| 3 | **The Waters** | Noah | River empires, cruelty spreading | *The Deluge*: an **optional** cleansing catastrophe. Triggering it purges corruption but costs heavy Agency and Joy |
| 4 | **The Covenant** | Abraham | Nomads, kingdoms, idols | *The Altar*: a test where the people expect a sacrifice from you or from them |
| 5 | **The Law** | Moses | Empires, slavery, written law | *The Wilderness*: a freed people wander; Command is strong but Agency bleeds |
| 6 | **The Meridian** | Christ | Classical empires, philosophy, roads | *The Rejection*: your greatest Witness is turned on by the people |
| 7 | **Drawn from a pool** (see below) | varies | varies | varies |
| 8 | **The Fulness** | Restoration, latter days | Industrial to modern, global networks | *The Last Crisis*: humanity's capacity to destroy itself peaks |

**Dispensation 7 is drawn from a pool each run.** Launch pool:
- **The Other Fold** (internal ref: Lehi, scattered branches). Scattered civilizations on separate continents. Trial: *The Dwindling*, where branches lose contact and records.
- **The Great Apostasy** (internal ref: the long dark age). Truth decays across the world; religions harden into tradition. Trial: *The Long Night*, where Reveal cards are weakened until a Restoration is triggered.
More entries can join the pool later, and the people model can weight which one appears based on the world's history.

**The final crisis is procedural.** The Last Crisis draws from the world's own history. A science-forward world may face runaway technology; a high-Command world may face total tyranny; a low-Truth world may face mass nihilism; a war-torn world may face annihilation. The people model decides which threat is ripe (doc 05).

## Endless: beyond the Fulness (4a)

After Dispensation 8 you can continue for **four more levels**. Change is much harder and needs large combos.

1. **The Spirit World.** Everyone who ever lived in this world returns. Temples you built across the run turn into multipliers. Your goal is to raise Redemption among the dead.
2. **Telestial.** Lift the least-redeemed. Cheapest gains, but they are hard to move.
3. **Terrestrial.** The honorable but unvaliant. Requires Truth combos.
4. **Celestial.** Perfection of the faithful. Requires near-perfect suit synergy.

Your final grade is the glory tier your world reaches, set by the God Score (doc 04).

## Opposition (2c)

There is **no mandatory adversary NPC.** Opposition comes from three sources:

1. **Human choice.** The people model can choose pride, violence, greed and control. This is the main opponent and it improves across runs.
2. **Entropy.** Buildings decay, records are lost, institutions ossify, truth drifts into tradition.
3. **The Shadow (optional Power).** You can purchase a shadow version of yourself as a Godhood Power. While active it:
   - increases suffering (Joy −) and spawns tempters among the people;
   - **requires more faith early**, but every person who stays faithful under that pressure counts for more Redemption (opposition gives choice its weight);
   - can be dismissed later in the run, at a cost.
   It is a high-risk, high-reward build, never a requirement.

**The opposite trap.** If you prize only Joy, Progress and Knowledge and never reveal, the people prosper but listen less and condemn themselves more. Redemption stalls. The game should make this feel like a real philosophical tension, not a punishment.

## Narrative delivery

- **No cutscenes.** The story is told through Witness visions (short card-flavor text), Chronicle entries written by the people ("In the third year of the river kings…"), and the final Record of your world.
- **The Chronicle** is your world's history, written in the voice of its people. It is generated within strict templates (doc 05) and saved to the Records.
- **The Testament.** Each run, your people write their own scripture: books attributed to their Witnesses, built from your Reveal plays, their doctrines and their Chronicle. Its accuracy reflects the world's Truth dial, so a corrupt world writes a corrupt scripture. It is readable at any time and kept in the Records after the run. Generated within the same bounded system as the Chronicle (doc 05), never quoting real scripture.
